The Children of Huang Shi — Where the Story Was Set

4 real historical locations · China

About

Based on the true story of British journalist George Hogg, who during the Second Sino-Japanese War took charge of an orphanage and led sixty orphaned children on a 700-mile trek across the Gobi Desert to safety.

CinemaMapped has mapped 4 real historical locations where the story takes place, spanning China.

Huang Shi, Hubei Province, China

George Hogg takes charge of a war orphanage at Huang Shi, caring for over 60 children while the Japanese advance threatens. He transforms the orphanage into ...

The real George Hogg ran the Baili Boys School in Shandan (not Huang Shi as the film depicts). He cared for approximately 60 orphaned boys and was helped by New Zealander Rewi Alley.

Shandan, Gansu Province, China (escape destination)

Hogg leads the sixty children on a 1,000-kilometre trek across mountains to safety in remote Shandan in Gansu Province, escaping the advancing Japanese front.

The real trek to Shandan in 1945 covered mountain terrain across the Qilian range. George Hogg died of tetanus in Shandan in July 1945, weeks after completing the journey. He was 30.
